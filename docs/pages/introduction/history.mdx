# History

[Grassroots Economics Foundation](https://grassecon.org) (GEF) developed its work through years of living and learning alongside communities in Kenya that sustained mutual care through shared commitments. Long before digital systems, people made promises to one another and held them through common memory, relationships, and local accountability. The book [Grassroots Economics: Reflection and Practice](https://willruddick.substack.com/p/grassroots-economics-the-book-is) describes this as a commons of commitments: promises that can be exchanged, fulfilled, and renewed without requiring one central authority.

That work was first expressed through community currencies—local ways to record and honor obligations when national currency was scarce. The deeper lesson was not a particular currency. It was a pattern of curation, shared valuation, limits that protect a commons, and exchange that preserves reciprocity. Those lessons informed the Commitment Pooling Protocol.

## From the historic Sarafu Network to Cosmo-Local Credit

The historic Sarafu Network served as a practical learning environment for community currencies and commitment pools across communities, schools, clinics, farms, cooperatives, and other local organizations. GEF used that experience to explore how independently governed commitments could be connected without collapsing them into a single dominant market or removing local responsibility.

The service formerly presented as Sarafu Network has migrated to the Cosmo-Local Credit progressive web app at [cosmolocal.credit](https://cosmolocal.credit), which GEF operates. The migration is a transition of the public service and platform. It is not a representation that every historic Sarafu account, Wallet, Token, Voucher, Pool, balance, report, or transaction was transferred, preserved, or remains available in the current App.

Cosmo-Local Credit carries this work forward as both a live application and a broader protocol for redeemable commitments and curated Pools. Issuers remain responsible for what they promise, Pool Stewards remain responsible for their rules and any guarantees they publish, and different communities and institutions can use governance arrangements suited to their circumstances. GEF's operation of the public App does not automatically make it an Issuer, Pool Steward, guarantor, or party to transactions between users.

One path, in brief:

1. Community currencies made local obligations visible when cash was scarce.
2. Commitment pooling developed curation, valuation, limitation, and exchange into reusable protocol components.
3. The historic Sarafu Network tested and refined those ideas across many real communities and organizations.
4. The public service transitioned to Cosmo-Local Credit, while the protocol generalized the model for independently governed Vouchers and Pools.
5. The current PWA makes supported Wallet, Voucher, Pool, and Swap workflows available at `cosmolocal.credit`; wider routing, clearing, liquidity, insurance, and governance mechanisms remain deployment-specific.

The [Terms of Service](/governance/terms) govern use of the public App. The [White Paper](/white-paper) describes the wider design, research, and roadmap.

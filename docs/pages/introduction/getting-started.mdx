# Getting started

Cosmo-Local Credit (CLC) helps people describe what they offer, publish what they need, and exchange vouchers through independently governed Commitment Pools. Open the [CLC App](https://cosmolocal.credit) to begin.

The basic idea is simple:

1. Create a voucher—much like a gift card—for something you can provide.
2. Publish an Offering so people can discover the related goods or services.
3. Publish or respond to a Want to find possible exchange partners.
4. Use a Commitment Pool to exchange supported vouchers under published rules.
5. Use a voucher with its Issuer for what its terms promise.

“Credit” in the App means recognized commitments and Voucher exchange capacity. It is not automatically a personal loan, borrowing limit, or promise to advance money.

For exact definitions, see [Concepts and vocabulary](/introduction/concepts).

## A first journey through the App

### 1. Explore the Market and Wants

Browse the [CLC App](https://cosmolocal.credit) before creating anything. The **Market** is a discovery catalog, not automatically a seller, exchange venue, endorsement, or guarantee.

When reviewing a Voucher, check:

- who issued it;
- the goods or services it promises;
- where, when, and how it can be used;
- its limits, expiry, contact method, and complaint process; and
- which Commitment Pools support it.

A **Want** expresses demand. Publishing or answering one does not automatically accept a Voucher, reserve goods, authorize an exchange, or create an obligation. Contacting or messaging another participant also does not by itself create, fulfill, or discharge a Voucher commitment.

### 2. Create or access an account and Wallet

The App uses an account for profile and session access and a **Wallet** for blockchain authorization. Protect your passkey, paper-wallet material, recovery information, devices, and any other credentials. Review every address, asset, amount, Pool, fee, and authorization before confirming a transaction.

The interface asks you to confirm the Terms during onboarding and before publishing a Voucher or Pool. Read the [Terms of Service](/governance/terms) and [Privacy Notice](/governance/privacy) before continuing.

### 3. Create a Voucher and Offering

Create a Voucher for something you can reasonably provide. Think of it as making your own gift card: you describe what it represents and the conditions for using it.

A complete Voucher has several separate layers:

1. token-contract mechanics and balances;
2. the Issuer's published Voucher terms;
3. one or more **Offering** records describing associated goods or services; and
4. the Issuer's real-world fulfillment.

Creating or minting a token does not prove that an Offering exists, that the Issuer has capacity, or that Grassroots Economics Foundation (GEF) guarantees fulfillment.

### 4. Publish or respond to a Want

Use a Want to describe something you are looking for, including the amount, place, timing, and a suitable contact method. Another participant can discover it, review your public Vouchers and Offerings, and decide whether to contact you.

Keep negotiation and responsibility clear. A message can help participants agree on delivery, booking, or other details, but the applicable Voucher terms and any separately agreed transaction terms determine the commitment.

### 5. Find or create a Commitment Pool

A Commitment Pool curates selected Vouchers and enables their exchange. Before using or supporting one, review:

- the accountable **Pool Steward** and current contract owner;
- admitted Vouchers and other supported assets;
- exchange-rate or quote methods;
- the App's **“Credit limit”**, which is a Pool token-balance cap rather than personal credit;
- Pool and protocol fees;
- available inventory and the Pool owner's liquidity-withdrawal power;
- proxy, multisig, and dependency controllers; and
- any separately published contribution, guarantee, reserve, reward, or insurance terms.

Depositing transfers assets into the Pool's `SwapPool`. Protocol v1.1.0 does not automatically give a depositor Pool shares, repayment, withdrawal, reward, or governance rights.

### 6. Send or exchange Vouchers

Use **Send** to transfer a Voucher directly. Use a **Pool swap** to exchange one supported asset for another through one Pool.

The App's **Credit** view can also find Vouchers reachable through several independently governed Pools. Choose **Get** for the Voucher you want or **Spend** for the Voucher you want to use. Then review:

1. the **Pay with** choice and **Estimate**;
2. **Route details**, including every Pool, amount, Pool fee, and protocol fee;
3. the minimum amount received and quote expiry; and
4. the prepared Wallet batch before choosing **Confirm exchange**.

Production routing is intentionally narrower than direct Pool swapping. It uses verified, relative-priced Pools and tokens whose transfers move exactly the stated amount: `GiftableToken` vouchers and explicitly allowlisted plain ERC-20 tokens. The public deployment searches routes of up to three Pool swaps and supports compatible EOA/Calibur Wallets, not Safes or other contract Wallets.

Route discovery is an estimate. Before signing, CLC Core reads the selected Pools at one pinned block, calculates a time-bounded quote, applies minimum-output and deadline bounds, and simulates the complete batch. If any call fails, the complete same-chain transaction reverts. The receipt groups the route's settled amounts and per-hop Pool and protocol fees.

### 7. Use a Voucher with its Issuer

Read the Voucher terms and follow the Issuer's booking, delivery, or contact process. In the current App, **“Redeem”** opens the ordinary send flow with the token owner's address prefilled. Sending the Voucher is redemption presentment; it does not prove that the Issuer has provided the promised goods or services.

After fulfillment, the Issuer should record **discharge** so fulfilled units cannot be used again—for example by receiving, burning, cancelling, or otherwise disabling them under the published terms.

## Before you publish

Voucher, Pool, Offering, Want, Catalog, Post, and Review information can have different visibility. Before publishing a resource:

- choose a map point that is safe to make public; exact coordinates may be visible;
- check which information is visible while signed out and while signed in;
- use contact details that are suitable for the intended audience;
- do not copy private profile information into a public resource unless you intend to publish it; and
- read the Privacy Notice's [“What becomes public?”](/governance/privacy#what-becomes-public) section.

## Current App actions

| App action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Send** | Transfers a token balance to another address. |
| **Pool swap** | Exchanges supported assets through one `SwapPool`. On-chain settlement does not prove Issuer fulfillment. |
| **Routed exchange** | Executes an ordered set of Pool swaps in one bounded, same-chain Wallet transaction. It does not merge the Pools' governance. |
| **“Redeem”** | Prefills a send to the token owner as redemption presentment. Fulfillment and discharge remain separate. |
| **“Request retirement”** | Lets the current owner ask an administrator to retire a Voucher or Pool. The resource remains active while the request is pending; the owner can cancel the request, and an administrator can approve or reject it. Retirement does not delete contracts, balances, or history. |
| **Pool retirement** | After approval, blocks new Pool swaps and deposits while keeping available withdrawals possible. It does not erase prior settlement records. |
| **Pool liquidity withdrawal** | Lets the Pool owner transfer available inventory to a selected address. |
| **Fee collection** | Sends accrued Pool fees to the configured recipient. |

## Responsibilities and important limits

[Grassroots Economics Foundation](https://grassrootseconomics.org/about/) operates the App and supporting infrastructure. Unless GEF expressly accepts another role for a particular transaction, it is not the Issuer of a user-created Voucher, the Steward of a user-created Pool, a custodian, lender, borrower, guarantor, insurer, or redeemer.

Before transacting, remember:

- You must meet the Terms' age, legal-capacity, and jurisdiction requirements.
- Issuers are responsible for their Voucher commitments.
- Pool Stewards are responsible for their published Pool rules and any guarantee they expressly assume.
- A listing, review, route, limit, reserve, or blockchain record is not automatically an endorsement, valuation, fulfillment record, guarantee, insurance policy, or promise of cash-out.
- Payment services, rewards, contribution arrangements, and other provider integrations are deployment-dependent and require their own disclosures.

Protocol v1.1.0 provides direct Pool execution and a quote-only Solidity `SwapRouter`. The production App and CLC Core coordinate same-chain multi-Pool execution above those contracts. Cross-chain routing, automatic Pool rebalancing, batch netting, shared insurance, proposed governance assets, network liquidity programs, and regulated payment rails remain proposed or deployment-dependent unless a specific deployment identifies them as active.
